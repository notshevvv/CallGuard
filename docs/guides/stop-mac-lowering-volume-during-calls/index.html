How to stop your Mac lowering music during calls

Updated 16 November 2026 · by notshevvv, maker of CallGuard

macOS lowers other audio during calls because the call app runs Apple's voice processing, which "ducks" every other sound on the same output. There is no switch for it in System Settings. The free app CallGuard keeps media at full volume by moving the call to a muted device, where the ducking lands harmlessly, and playing everything to your earbuds.

Why your music gets quieter when a call starts

Call apps such as WhatsApp use Apple's voice-processing audio unit to cancel echo. While it runs, it lowers ("ducks") all other audio playing on the same output device. How much it lowers is a setting inside that audio unit, and only the call app can change it. In our tests, the difference between the call engine's minimum and default ducking was 19 dB, enough to turn a song into background noise.

That's why searching System Settings doesn't help: the setting isn't yours to change. Re-playing the sound through another app doesn't escape it either. Audio played by a second app on the same device was ducked by exactly the same amount.

Fix it with CallGuard (free)

  1. Download CallGuard, unzip it, and drag it into Applications. On first launch, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  2. Click the mic icon in the menu bar and turn on No Ducking.
  3. Allow "System Audio Recording" when macOS asks. CallGuard uses it to take each app's sound before it is ducked. Nothing is recorded or saved.

During a call, CallGuard points the call app at the Mac's built-in speakers, muted in hardware, so the ducking lands on a device nobody is listening to. It then plays everything (your music and the other person's voice) to your earbuds from a copy taken before the drop. Your volume keys keep working, and everything switches back when the call ends.

Good to know

  • It needs earbuds or headphones, because the Mac's own speakers are the decoy.
  • Verified with WhatsApp for Mac. It should work with any app that follows the system output (FaceTime, Zoom, Teams, Meet), but these are untested. If an app doesn't switch, CallGuard falls back to boosting your media instead.
  • The other person never hears your music: CallGuard only changes what you hear.

On Windows? It has a built-in switch: see the Windows guide.

Quick answers

Is there a setting in macOS to stop ducking during calls?

No. The amount of ducking is set by the call app's own voice processing, and macOS has no user setting for it.

Will the other person hear my music?

No. CallGuard only changes what you hear. Your microphone isn't mixed with your media.

Does No Ducking work on the MacBook speakers?

No. The built-in speakers are used as the muted decoy, so No Ducking needs earbuds, headphones or another output.
